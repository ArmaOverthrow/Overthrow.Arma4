> Re-filed: originally investigated and written up under the BUG-116 number on 2026-08-09; a concurrent session claimed BUG-116 for the persistence duplicate-id bug, so this report moved here. Section B was reworded the same day after the BUG-105 runtime disproof (the scarcity term is a smooth curve, not a step — the collapse mechanism and every exploit consequence are unchanged).

## How this was found

Community report, reproduced by the reporter as a working exploit against the shipped `economy/shop-ux` build (menu Sell All + vehicle trunk "Sell Cargo Here"). Every step of the reporter's arithmetic checks out against the source. Their observed numbers — buy watches ~$50 at import, sell $85 inland, "$35 profit per watch", buy-back at $2 — are each reproduced by the formulas below.

## Symptom

Selling N items in one bulk sale credits `N × (unit price at pre-sale stock)`. The price the shop pays never responds to the quantity being dumped, no matter how large. Combined with two pre-existing pricing properties this closes an unbounded money loop:

1. **Import at a port** costs flat base price into a vehicle — no profit margin, no location term.
2. **Trunk-sell inland** pays base + scarcity term + an *uncapped* port-distance markup, for the entire cargo, in one RPC, at the pre-sale price.
3. **Buy back at ~$2**: the dumped stock drives the scarcity term far below zero (unbounded, see B), the sell price clamps to $1, so the buy price becomes $2 — the player repurchases ~96% of the goods for pennies and repeats at the next shop.

Shops have no treasury; `DoAddPlayerMoney` mints the payout from nothing, so this is pure currency inflation, plus the reported secondary effect of thousands of stock units per shop ("things can get insane/laggy").

### Reporter's loop, priced by the code

Watch: base price `GetPrice(id)` = $50, inland shop ~7 km from the nearest port, town stock at or above max (scarcity term ≈ 0):

| Step | Code path | Price each |
|---|---|---|
| Import 10,000 at port into vehicle | `RpcAsk_ImportToVehicle`: `cost = qty * GetPrice(id)` | $50 → −$500,000 |
| Trunk Sell All inland | `ExecuteSell`: `50 + ~0 + 50·7000·0.0001` | $85 → +$850,000 |
| Buy back 9,636 | `GetBuyPrice` over collapsed sell price | $2 → −$19,272 |
| Drive to next town, repeat | fresh per-town stock (`GetTownStock` is per town) | +$819,060 … |

`50·7000·0.0001 = 35` — exactly the reporter's "$35 profit per watch"; `50 + 35 = 85` — exactly their sell price.

## Root cause

Four independently-owned properties compose; the first is the one this feature shipped.

### A. `ExecuteSell` prices the entire batch at pre-sale stock (`economy/shop-ux`)

`Scripts/Game/Components/Controller/OVT_ShopTransactionComponent.c`:

- `ExecuteSell` (`:281-380`) resolves the unit price through `ResolveUnitPrice` (`:460-471`), which **memoises per resource id for the whole sale** (`unitPrices` map, `:303`, cache hit at `:462`).
- Even without the memo the result is identical, because `RestockShop` (`:561-575`) mutates `shop.m_aInventory` only **after** the loop (`:372`) — `GetSellPrice`'s `GetTownStock` reading cannot see the stock being added by the sale in progress.

The feature's own design (implementation.md D4) required the *quantity* be server-clamped, and it is; nothing required the *price* to respond to quantity. Both bulk entry points share this: menu Sell All (`RpcAsk_SellItems`, `maxItems = quantity`) and the trunk path (`RpcAsk_SellVehicleCargo`, `filterResourceId = -1`, `maxItems = -1`, whole cargo). The trunk path is the force multiplier — menu sales are bounded by carry capacity, a vehicle is not.

Note the legacy deleted `RpcAsk_Sell` had the same flat-price-per-call shape; what shop-ux changed is that *one call can now carry an entire vehicle*, so the stock feedback that exists is bypassed entirely for arbitrarily large dumps.

### B. The scarcity term is unbounded below and collapses the price to $1 on overstock (`economy/market`)

`OVT_EconomyManagerComponent.GetSellPrice` (`:537-557`):

```c
price = Math.Round(price + ((1 - ((float)stock_level / max_stock)) * (price * 0.1)) + (price * distance_to_port * 0.0001));
if(price < 0) price = 1;
```

The term is a smooth curve (runtime-verified 2026-08-09 — see BUG-105's resolution finding; the earlier "integer-division step" framing was disproven, and `OVT_TEST_Campaign_Economy_ScarcityPriceGradient` now pins the gradient). Within the design band it spans only base+10% (empty town) to base+0% (full town) — but **below** the full mark it keeps going: at `stock = k·max_stock` the term is `(1 − k)·0.1·base`, so every additional `max_stock` of overstock shaves another 10% of base off the price, without bound, until the `price < 0` clamp lands at $1 (a price of exactly $0 survives the `< 0` test).

- `GetBuyPrice` (`:565-577`) = `Round(sellPrice · 1.25)` (margin default `0.25`, `OVT_OverthrowConfigComponent.c:167`), with a `buy == price → buy += 1` bump — sell $1 ⇒ **buy $2**. This is the reporter's "$2 from N stock and beyond" (the exact threshold depends on the town's `max_stock` and port distance).

So overstocking doesn't refuse the goods or hold the price — it hands them back nearly free. (Side effect: at a $1/$0 collapsed price, `ExecuteSell`'s `unitPrice <= 0` gate can make items unsellable while they remain buyable for $1–2.)

### C. Port import is flat base price; the port-distance markup is uncapped and one-sided (`economy/market`)

- `RpcAsk_ImportToVehicle` (`OVT_PlayerCommsComponent.c:776-860`) charges `qty * economy.GetPrice(id)` (`:842`) — the raw base price. No `m_fShopProfitMargin`, no location term. (Per-call `qty` cap is 100, `:781` — a click bound, not an economic one.)
- `GetSellPrice`'s port term `price · distance · 0.0001` grows without limit — +1% of base per 100 m. Any shop away from a port pays more for goods than the port sells them for; the spread `scarcity + base·d·0.0001` is guaranteed profit before the buy-back trick even starts. Requires the Trade-tree `Import` permission, i.e. mid-game, not end-game.

### D. Shops accept unlimited quantity with no capital or demand constraint

`ExecuteSell` → `RestockShop` adds any amount to `shop.m_aInventory`; nothing caps stock at `GetTownMaxStock` or scales acceptance by demand, and the payout is minted (`DoAddPlayerMoney`, no shop treasury). Excess stock decays only via the slow NPC-buy tick (`NPCTransactions`) — though shop stock is deliberately not persisted, so a server restart resets it.

## Fix (proposed — needs a gameplay owner's sign-off; several knobs, not all required)

The minimal exploit-killing set is probably 1 + 3; 2 and 4 are balance hardening.

1. **Price bulk sales marginally** (`shop-ux`, `OVT_ShopTransactionComponent`): inside `ExecuteSell`, account the stock being added as the loop runs — e.g. drop the memo and apply per-unit restock before pricing the next unit, or compute a closed-form average over the quantity. Since the scarcity curve is real (BUG-105 disproven), this is directly effective: a dump's later units price progressively lower.
2. **Cap the scarcity term's downside** (`market`): clamp the bracket to something like `[-0.5, +0.1]·price` (or clamp effective `stock_level` at a small multiple of `max_stock`) so overstock cannot collapse the price to $1 — this is what makes buy-back cheap. Alternatively (or additionally) keep `GetBuyPrice ≥ cost-of-acquisition` so a shop never resells below what it just paid.
3. **Close the import arbitrage** (`market`): price imports with the same margin shops charge (`GetPrice · (1 + m_fShopProfitMargin)` at minimum), and/or cap the port-distance term. As shipped, import→inland-sell is risk-free profit for any registered item, independent of A/B/D.
4. **Bound what a shop will buy** (`shop-ux` or `market`): refuse purchases that would push town stock beyond some multiple of `GetTownMaxStock` (counted into `skippedCount`, so the toast/hint stays honest). This kills the 10,000-watch dump wholesale and keeps `m_aInventory`/UI sizes sane (the reported lag).

## Fix implemented (2026-08-09) — knobs 1 + 4; pending play-test

**What shipped:** marginal batch pricing (the reported defect — "no accounting for reduced value with each item sold") plus a hard buy cap at 2× town max stock (gameplay owner's pick, 2026-08-09).

- `OVT_EconomyManagerComponent.GetSellPriceAtOffset(id, pos, stockOffset)` — new seam: the sell price as it would be after `stockOffset` additional units enter the town's stock. `GetSellPrice` now delegates to it with offset 0 (behaviour unchanged for every existing caller).
- `OVT_ShopTransactionComponent.ExecuteSell` — the per-sale price memo is gone; unit *i* of a resource is priced with offset *i* (the `collated` count of units already deleted this call), through the same `ResolveUnitPrice` the sell browser's display path uses.
- `OVT_EconomyManagerComponent.CanTownAbsorbStock(townId, id, extraUnits)` + `TOWN_STOCK_BUY_CAP_MULTIPLIER = 2` — knob 4: shops refuse units that would push town stock past 2× `GetTownMaxStock`. `ExecuteSell` gates every unit on it (passing the collated count as `extraUnits`, so one Sell All cannot blow through the cap before the stock broadcast lands, and refusals count into `skippedCount`); `ShopBuysResource` checks it too, so saturated rows grey out in the sell browser with the existing not-bought-here reason.

**Why this kills the loop** (knobs 2/3 left as optional balance levers):

- *Recycling is now a guaranteed loss:* selling Q units earns the integral of the curve going down; buying them back costs the same integral × 1.25 margin going up. The exploit only worked because the sale paid the flat top-of-curve price while buy-back paid the collapsed bottom.
- *The collapsed-price regime is unreachable:* the 2× cap stops acceptance while the marginal price is still ~90% of base + port term, far above the $1 clamp — so the $2 buy-back state cannot be created by selling at all, and shop inventories stay sane (the reported lag).
- *The "pre-buy to maximise the price" step dies:* the first units sold immediately re-fill what the pre-buy drained.
- *Import haul-trade survives deliberately:* buying at the port and hauling a truckload inland still pays — bounded per town by the curve and the cap, which is the intended trading gameplay. Capping the port-distance term or margining imports (knobs 2/3) remain available as balance levers but are not needed to close the exploit.

**Display note:** the sell browser's unit price is the marginal first-unit price; a large Sell All credits the curve sum, which is less than `unit × count`. The toast/hint reports the actual amount credited, so the feedback stays honest.

**Verification:** compile-check clean. Two new Campaign-tier cases, each proven red once:
- `OVT_TEST_Campaign_Economy_SellPriceStockOffset` — offset-k over stock-s must equal the plain price at stock s+k; proven red by mutating `+ stockOffset → + 0` (`offset 99 priced 1116 but stocking 100 prices 1018`).
- `OVT_TEST_Campaign_Economy_TownBuyCap` — cap flips exactly at 2× max (derived from the constant, not hardcoded) and extras count; proven red by the same `+ extraUnits → + 0` mutation (`stock 101 and 101 extra units against a cap of 202` wrongly absorbed).

Full All group 71/71 green.

## Notes

- **Relationship to BUG-105:** BUG-105's diagnosed integer-division step was disproven at runtime (2026-08-09) — the curve works. That did *not* close this exploit: the curve was still flat for the whole of any single bulk sale (A), still unbounded below until the $1 clamp (B), and imports are still flat-priced (C).
- The sell browser and `RpcDo_SellResult` display path are honest about what was paid — the displayed unit price (`GetSellUnitPrice`) and the credited price share `ResolveUnitPrice`, so client and server agree. The problem is the price model, not a display/authority drift.
- `RpcAsk_Buy` re-derives the price per RPC (`GetShopBuyPrice`, `OVT_PlayerCommsComponent.c:617`), so the buy-back leg has no additional batching defect; the UI buys one unit per click.
- Not touched here: `RpcAsk_SellDrugs` (separate path, same flat-price-per-call shape — worth a look when this is fixed).
